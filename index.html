How this was tested

  • Data. Daily closing values and Total Return Index (price plus reinvested dividends) from NSE Indices for Nifty 50, Nifty Next 50 and Nifty Midcap 150. Nifty Midcap 150 starts in April 2005, which still leaves enough history for a 200-day average before the 20-year window opens.
  • The signal. 200-trading-day simple average of the price index. On each investment day the rule looks at the previous day's close, so it never uses a price it couldn't have known, and trades at that day's close.
  • Checked monthly. The rule only acts on investment days. A crash that starts and ends between two investment days is not avoided.
  • FD. 6% a year, compounded daily. Post-tax, FD interest is taxed at 31.2% (30% slab plus cess) as it accrues.
  • Equity tax. Post-tax uses today's rules across the whole history: 20% on gains held under a year, 12.5% above that with ₹1.25 lakh exempt per financial year. The switch pays this every time it exits. Older periods actually had lower rates, so this is a what-if.
  • Left out. Fund expense ratio (about 0.1–0.3% a year on an index fund), tracking error, exit loads and stamp duty. These would trim every equity strategy a little and the switch a little more.

Other strategies worth testing

  • Valuation-based allocation. Shift between index and debt by Nifty P/E or P/B bands, such as more equity when P/E is below its long-run median. NSE publishes daily P/E, P/B and dividend yield, so this can run on the same data.
  • Step-up SIP. Raise the monthly amount by 10% each year. It usually matters more for the final corpus than any timing rule.
  • Buy the dip top-up. Keep the regular SIP and add an extra lump sum whenever the index is 10%, 20% or 30% below its high, funded from a liquid fund.
  • Value averaging. Invest whatever it takes to keep the portfolio on a target growth path, so you buy more after falls and less after rallies.
  • Fixed asset mix with rebalancing. For example 70% index and 30% FD or debt, rebalanced once a year or when the mix drifts 5% from target.
  • Trend filter variants. 50/200-day crossover, a 10-month average on month-end closes, or acting only after two closes below the average. These test how much of the whipsaw cost here comes from the exact rule.
  • Dual momentum. Each month hold whichever of Nifty 50, Next 50 and Midcap 150 has the best 6 or 12-month return, and go to debt if none beats the FD rate.
  • Index mix. Split the SIP across all three indices, such as 50/25/25, and compare return per unit of drawdown with any single index.
